# ButterFold LVS diagnostic: 11-vs-21 root cause

> Superseded boundary status (2026-08-11): the physical defect documented
> below has been repaired. See `PAD_BOUNDARY_CONNECTIVITY_REPAIR_REPORT.md`.
> The original evidence remains here to preserve provenance.

## Executive conclusion

The original failure is **not** caused by missing GDS text labels and the 11
extracted terminals are **not** the eleven ButterFold inputs.  Both routed DEFs
and the candidate GDS preserve all 21 signal names.  The failure is physical
connectivity at the pad boundary:

- every top-level external net has a BTerm and pad `PAD` ITerm in OpenDB;
- none of the 21 external nets has a routed `dbWire`;
- the ten west-side labels happen to overlap hierarchical bond-pad metal, so
  KLayout attaches them to pad circuits;
- the nine east-side output labels and two north-side clock/reset labels touch
  only isolated top-cell port rectangles, not hierarchical pad metal;
- KLayout therefore extracts 10 logical signal terminals plus global substrate
  `SUB`, for 11 total.

This is the first divergence.  It exists in the authoritative routed ODB/DEF,
not in ODB-to-DEF conversion or label export.  Adding labels cannot repair it;
a future physical repair must connect the east/north BTerm shapes to their pad
`PAD` shapes (or place them so legal overlap exists), then reroute and
re-stream.  That physical modification is outside this task's frozen scope.

Consequently, top-pin checkpoint A remains **10/21 logical signals**, and it
would be technically invalid to waive pins or force a library abstraction to
claim full LVS success.  The candidate GDS hash remains unchanged.

## 6. Working and failing traces

### Working: `pad_din[0]`

1. Physical Verilog: `u_pad_din0.PAD(pad_din[0])`.
2. Both DEFs: input BTerm on Metal3 at the west edge, connected logically to
   `u_pad_din0/PAD`; no routed wire.
3. GDS: label `pad_din[0]` on 42/10 at (0.26, 617.40) um.
4. At that point the audit finds the top pin rectangle **and** hierarchical
   `Bondpad_5LM` Metal3 geometry.
5. The deck connects `metal3_con` to `metal3_label`; KLayout creates the
   top-level terminal and connects it to the input pad.

### Failing: `pad_dout[0]`

1. Physical Verilog: `u_pad_dout0.PAD(pad_dout[0])`.
2. Both DEFs: inout BTerm on Metal3 at the east edge, logically associated
   with `u_pad_dout0/PAD`; no routed wire.
3. GDS: label `pad_dout[0]` on 42/10 at (2234.74, 542.36) um.
4. The point touches only the tiny top pin rectangle.  No hierarchical pad
   metal reaches it.  The placed output-pad bounding box ends near 2210 um,
   leaving about 24.74 um to the label at the 2235 um die edge.
5. The isolated labeled shape has no extracted pad device connection, so it is
   purged/not emitted as a top circuit terminal.

`pad_dout_valid_o` fails identically.  `pad_din_ready_o` uses the same `bi_t`
cell but is on the west side, where its label overlaps bond-pad metal, proving
that direction and the `bi_t` master itself are not what decides top-terminal
recognition.

`pad_clk` and `pad_rst_n` fail by the same geometry mechanism on the north
side.  Their north-pad bounding boxes end near y=2210 um while labels are at
y=2234.74 um.

## 7. LVS deck interpretation

The installed deck defines:

- Metal1 label: 34/10;
- Metal2 label: 36/10;
- Metal3 label: 42/10;
- Metal4 label: 46/10;
- Metal5 label: 81/10.

`rule_decks/general_connections.lvs` explicitly connects each conductive
metal layer to its label layer.  A label must touch the connected conductive
region to name that region and become a meaningful terminal.  The candidate's
21 labels use correct layers; eleven logical labels are attached only to
isolated port rectangles.

## 8. Root cause and GDS decision

The first loss point is **route.odb / route.def physical connectivity**:
BTerms and names are present, but no external pad net has routing.  The pad
placement/orientation makes west-side shapes overlap by coincidence/design,
while east/north shapes do not.

This task did not extend metal or move pads because that would modify frozen
routed geometry.  Stream-out correctly reproduces the authoritative DEF and
all label text.  Adding duplicate labels, detached labels, or stream-out-only
metal would conceal rather than repair the physical disconnection.

```text

NEW GDS SHA-256: unchanged (no GDS regeneration justified)
TOP SIGNAL TERMINALS AFTER INFRASTRUCTURE DIAGNOSTIC: 10 / 21
```

## 9. `bi_t` connectivity audit

All ten physical `bi_t` instances connect their output data to `A`, force
`OE=1`, `IE=0`, select `PDRV0=PDRV1=1`, disable pulls (`PU=PD=0`), set
`SL=CS=0`, and connect the external BTerm net to `PAD`.  `Y` is unused, which
is appropriate for output-only use.  Their VDD/DVDD and VSS/DVSS pins are on
the common physical power nets.  No functional pad-wiring error was found in
the reference connectivity; the failure is the missing metal connection from
the top BTerm geometry to `PAD` geometry on east/north sides.

## 10. Power/ground model

The design has no VDD/VSS BTerms among the 21 signal ports.  Physical power is
represented through power-pad instances and two special/common nets:

- `one_`: VDD, VNW, and DVDD;
- `zero_`: VSS, VPW, and DVSS.

Standard-cell wells, SRAM VDD/VSS, and pad core/I/O supplies are connected by
global-connect rules and PDN/special nets.  The physical Verilog omits implicit
power pins; reference generation restores them deterministically from PDK pin
names and the same global-connect policy.

KLayout also extracts a global substrate terminal named `SUB`.  The installed
deck's `substrate_connections.lvs` globally connects substrate/taps to `SUB`.
The reference currently expresses ground as `zero_`, so final substrate-name
alignment remains **PROVISIONAL**.  Adding a logical VSS signal pin is neither
required nor justified by this evidence.

Representative deep-extraction results are:

| Cell | Layout pins | Reference pins | Observation |
|---|---:|---:|---|

| `mux2_1` | 8 | 8 | Layout has generated net/`SUB`; reference has `VNW`/`VPW` |
| `in_c` | 6 | 8 | Unused pull controls disappear from layout extraction; reference retains them |
| `bi_t` | 6 | 15 | Constant/unused control paths and `Y` disappear; reference retains all interface pins |
| SRAM256x8 | 37 | 37 | Same external pin count, reordered by extracted connectivity; internal hierarchy/device representation differs |

An isolated `buf_1` test using the installed library GDS, its installed SPICE
subcircuit, and the same PDK KLayout deck failed in both deep and flat modes.
This proves the leaf mismatch is not caused solely by ButterFold interconnect.
The PDK documentation describes transistor extraction but does not prescribe a
standard-cell/I/O/SRAM black-box list or pin-reduction policy for this mixed
hierarchical assembly.  Arbitrary black-boxing is therefore not justified.

The technically defensible policy at this checkpoint is:

| Family | Layout handling | Reference handling | Pin comparison | Internal device comparison | Status/reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ButterFold generated interconnect | Extract completely | Post-route physical Verilog | Required | N/A | BLOCKED by disconnected pad BTerms |
| Standard cells | Transistor extraction with PDK deck | Installed SPICE | Required | Required unless an official preverified boundary policy is identified | FAIL; well/substrate hierarchy mismatch |
| I/O pads | Transistor extraction with PDK deck | Installed I/O SPICE | Required, including PAD/supplies/controls | Required unless official abstraction exists | FAIL; extracted controls/pins collapse |
| SRAM | Preserve macro hierarchy and verify every external pin; full device compare only with supported hierarchy mapping | Installed SRAM SPICE/CDL | Mandatory for both instances | PROVISIONAL; no installed black-box prescription found | FAIL/not established |

No cell family and certainly not the ButterFold top was black-boxed merely to
obtain a green result.

## 12. Reference and bus-name audit

input isolation, pads, and both SRAM instances.  The generated reference uses
